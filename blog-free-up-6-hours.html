How to Free Up 6 Hours a Week Without Hiring Anyone

The hard part isn't deciding to do it. It's finding the hours.

Most guides stop at "spend 6 hours a week working on the business, not in it" — as if that's the hard part. It's not. This is how to actually find the time when your week is already full.

Delivery 4 min read

If you want to grow the practise, or improve margin, or both, something has to give in your week.

Most guides stop at "spend 6 hours a week working on the business, not in it" — as if that's the hard part.

It's not. The hard part is finding the 6 hours when your week is already full. This is how to actually do it.

The diary isn't the problem

Most Principals go looking for 6 hours by staring at an already-packed calendar, hoping a gap appears. It won't. The calendar isn't where the problem lives — it's just where the symptom shows up.

The real issue is that a Principal is still doing work that doesn't need a Principal doing it. That's not a criticism — in a small firm, it happens by default, not by decision. Nobody sat down and decided the Principal should be the one chasing documents or re-explaining a matter for the third time. It just ended up that way, one small task at a time, until it was normal.

Step 1: Track one week, honestly

Before you cut anything, log your actual time in 30-minute blocks for one working week. Not what you assume you do — what you actually do.

Most Principals are surprised by what they find. Usually it breaks down into three things:

  • Work a senior lawyer could be doing
  • Work an admin person could be doing
  • Work that shouldn't be happening at all — chasing information that should already be in the file, re-explaining something that should've been documented once and never needed repeating

Step 2: Sort it into three buckets

  • Only I can do this. Advice, negotiation, the actual practise of law, the key client relationships.
  • Someone else could do this, if it were set up properly. File reviews, routine correspondence, status updates, first-pass drafting.
  • This shouldn't be taking anyone's time. Duplicate data entry, hunting for documents, answering the same client question for the fifth time because there was never anywhere for them to check it themselves.

Most firms find their 6 hours in the second and third buckets — without touching the first one at all. Nobody's being asked to hand over the client relationships or the legal judgement. That's not where the leak is.

Step 3: Fix the third bucket first

This is the fastest win, because it doesn't need anyone's trust or training — it's a process fix, not a people fix.

The usual culprits: no standard file structure, so everyone searches a different way. No templates for the letters you send fifty times a year. No way for a client to check progress themselves, so they ring you instead. Matters re-explained at every handover because nothing was ever written down.

Fix the process and the time comes back on its own.

Step 4: Move the second bucket — deliberately

Delegation usually fails in small firms not because the staff can't do the work, but because it's handed over vaguely, and then pulled back the moment it's done a little differently to how the Principal would've done it.

Pick one recurring task. Write down exactly what "done" looks like — not "the way I'd do it," but the actual standard. Hand it to whoever's best placed for it. Check the result against that standard. Only move to the next task once this one is genuinely off your plate — not "mostly," genuinely.

Step 5: Protect the hours once you've found them

This is where most Principals lose the gains straight back. The 6 hours get found, and within a fortnight they've been quietly swallowed by the next urgent thing.

Block the time like a client appointment, because functionally that's what it is.

If it keeps getting bumped for other things, that's telling you something — the practise still treats the Principal's time as the release valve for everything else. That habit usually costs a lot more than 6 hours a week; it's just harder to see because it doesn't show up as a single line item.

Where to start

You don't need to overhaul the practise in one go. Track the week. Find the three buckets. Fix the process leaks first — they're the fastest to close and need nobody's buy-in but your own.
